# Offer Strategy Guide — Brampton/GTA

*Internal reference. Not for client distribution.*

---

## Pre-Offer Checklist

Before submitting any offer, confirm:

- [ ] Buyer pre-approval letter current (within 90 days, matches or exceeds purchase price)
- [ ] Down payment funds confirmed (bank statement or letter)
- [ ] BRA signed and in place
- [ ] CMA completed — know fair market value
- [ ] Property inspected or waiver decision made with client knowledge
- [ ] Title search initiated
- [ ] Deposit cheque prepared (certified, 3-5% of purchase price)
- [ ] Client reviewed key APS clauses

## Brampton Market Offer Patterns

### Offer Night (Most Common in Active Market)

**How it works:**
- Seller lists Tuesday/Wednesday
- No offers reviewed until offer night (Sunday or set date)
- All offers submitted by deadline (e.g., 7:00 PM Sunday)
- Seller reviews all offers simultaneously
- Usually 1-3 counter-offer rounds

**Strategy:**
- Research recent comparables — sold price vs. list price ratio
- In balanced market: list + 0-3%
- In seller's market (multiple offers expected): list + 3-8% depending on comp analysis
- Escalation clause option: auto-beat highest offer up to cap (use cautiously — reveals your ceiling)

**Deposit:** Submit certified cheque or bank draft to listing brokerage within 24h of acceptance. Typically 3-5%. Larger deposit = signal of serious buyer.

---

### Pre-Emptive Offer (Bully Offer)

Used to bypass offer night. Only appropriate when:
- Property clearly underpriced
- Likely to attract 5+ offers on offer night
- Client has strong conviction + financing certainty

**Mechanics:**
- Submit offer before offer night with irrevocable period of 2-3 hours
- Must be significantly above expected offer night range
- Usually unconditional (or tight conditions)
- Listing agent required to present to seller immediately

**Risk:** Seller may reject and wait for offer night anyway. You've shown your hand.

---

### Conditional Offers

Common in slower markets or unique properties. Typical conditions:

| Condition | Typical Period | Notes |
|-----------|---------------|-------|
| Financing | 5 business days | Lender review of property + buyer |
| Home inspection | 3-5 business days | Buyer's inspector |
| Status certificate | 10 days (condos only) | Review of condo docs |
| Sale of buyer's property | Negotiated | Seller may include 48h clause |

**48h clause:** If seller accepts conditional offer, they can continue to market. If new offer comes, buyer gets 48h notice to firm up or lose the deal.

## Agreement of Purchase and Sale (APS) — Key Clauses

### Deposit
- Amount and timing
- Who holds it (listing brokerage in trust)
- What happens to deposit if deal falls through

### Completion Date (Closing)
- Typically 30-90 days in Brampton
- Longer completions (90-120 days) sometimes preferred by sellers who need time to move
- First-time buyers may prefer 60 days for financing finalization

### Chattels and Fixtures
- Included: items that are permanently attached (light fixtures, built-ins, fridge, stove typically)
- Excluded: items specifically listed by seller (custom chandelier, basement fridge, etc.)
- **Review MLS listing carefully** — exclusions listed there are binding

### Schedule A (Buyer Conditions and Terms)
- All conditions go here
- Any special negotiated terms
- SPIS (Seller Property Information Statement) request if applicable

### Irrevocable Period
- How long seller has to accept/reject/counter
- Standard: next business day
- Hot market: 2-4 hours (creates urgency)

## Price Strategy Framework

### Comparative Market Analysis (CMA)

Pull comparables:
1. Same property type (detached vs. semi vs. townhouse)
2. Same neighbourhood (within 0.5km ideally)
3. Sold within 90 days (60 days in active market)
4. Similar square footage (±15%)
5. Similar age/condition

**Adjustments:**
- Lot size: $10-20k per 1,000 sq ft over/under comparable
- Basement suite (legal, sep entrance): +$50-80k
- Updated kitchen: +$20-40k
- Updated bathrooms: +$10-20k per bath
- Garage (attached vs. detached): +$20-30k

**Note:** These are general Brampton estimates. Always verify with recent sold data.

## Multiple Offers — How to Advise

When in a multiple offer situation:

1. **Share the CMA** — let buyer decide based on data, not emotion
2. **Clarify: how badly do they want THIS property vs. similar?**
3. **Scenario model:** "If we offer X and lose, how will you feel? If we offer Y and win but overpay $20k, how will you feel in 5 years?"
4. **Know their ceiling BEFORE the offer night** — don't decide in the heat of the moment

**Never:** Invent competing offers to pressure the buyer. RECO Code of Ethics violation.

**Do:** If client is in a competing offer, you can ask listing agent: "Can you confirm there are registered offers?" — they must answer honestly.

## Deposit Handling

**Legal requirements (TRESA):**
- Deposit held in trust by brokerage
- Cannot be released without mutual consent or court order
- If deal falls through due to buyer default — seller typically keeps deposit
- If deal falls through due to condition — deposit returned to buyer

**Common scenarios:**
- Condition not waived: deposit returned within 3-5 business days
- Mutual release signed: deposit released per terms of release
- Disputed deposit: held in trust pending resolution (can take months)

## Post-Offer — What Happens Next

1. **Acceptance** → Both parties sign → Become legally bound
2. **Condition period** → Buyer arranges inspection/financing
3. **Waiver or fulfilment** → Conditions removed = firm deal
4. **Title search** → Lawyer reviews title, confirms no liens
5. **Closing adjustments** → Property taxes, utilities prorated
6. **Closing day** → Funds transfer, keys released

**Key date:** Firm deal = both parties locked in. No exit without mutual consent or breach of contract.

## Red Flags — When to Pause

Advise client to pause and reconsider if:

- Property has multiple municipal work orders outstanding
- Environmental concerns (oil tank, industrial nearby)
- Seller refuses any home inspection (highly unusual for detached)
- Basement suite is unpermitted and seller won't negotiate on price
- Title shows registered right-of-way or easement not disclosed
- Property has been sold 3+ times in past 24 months (assignment flipping)
- Seller demands unusually short irrevocable (< 1 hour) — creates artificial pressure

**Note:** Agents must disclose all known material facts. If you uncover a red flag, document and disclose to client in writing.
