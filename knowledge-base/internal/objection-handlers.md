# Objection Handlers — Brampton Buyer Consultations

*Internal reference for agent and AI copilot. Not for client distribution.*

---

## Category 1: Commitment Objections

### "I'm just browsing / not ready yet"

**What it signals:** Early-stage buyer. May be 6-12 months out. Not urgent — but worth nurturing.

**Response:**
"That's totally fine — most of my best clients started that way. The market moves fast, and buyers who've done their research early tend to make much better decisions when they're ready. Can I ask — what's your rough timeline? Even a general sense helps me know what to send you."

**Follow-up action:** Add to early nurture sequence. Send Brampton affordability guide.

---

### "I need to think about it"

**Response:**
"Of course. What would help you feel more confident about the next step? Is it the financial side, the neighbourhood research, or something else? I want to make sure I'm giving you what you actually need."

**Key insight:** Don't let "think about it" end the conversation. Uncover the real hesitation.

---

### "We're waiting for the market to drop"

**Response:**
"That's a common instinct — and I understand it. Here's the reality in Brampton: the entry-level detached market has historically recovered faster than most people expect. The buyers who waited for the 2022 correction until 2023 still paid within 5% of 2022 prices for detached homes in Credit Valley. The real question is: do you want to own in 5 years? If yes, timing the market usually costs more than it saves."

**Note:** Never guarantee market direction. This is a general market education response, not financial advice.

## Category 2: Financial Objections

### "We can't afford Brampton"

**Response:**
"Let's figure that out together before we assume. What's your pre-approval or budget range? Brampton has options starting under $700k for semi-detached — and with a legal basement suite generating $1,400-$1,800/month, the effective carrying cost on a $800k home can be comparable to renting. Have you factored in rental income from a basement suite?"

**Brampton affordability frame:** Use basement income as an affordability bridge.

---

### "We don't have a down payment yet"

**Response:**
"That's actually more common than you'd think, and there are options depending on your situation. Do you have equity in another property, or is this your first home? There are First Home Savings Accounts (FHSA), HBP withdrawals from RRSP, and 5% minimum down on homes under $500k. I'd recommend speaking with a mortgage broker — they can show you exactly where you stand. Would it help if I connected you with someone I trust?"

**TRESA note:** Recommend independent mortgage advice. Do not steer toward a specific lender without proper disclosure.

---

### "The interest rates are too high right now"

**Response:**
"Rates are definitely higher than a few years ago — that's real. The calculation that works for many Brampton buyers: a legal basement suite offsets roughly $500-700/month in mortgage cost. At current rates on an $800k home, you're typically looking at $4,200-4,500/month — but with $1,600/month rental income, effective cost is around $2,600-2,900. That's often competitive with renting a comparable house. Want me to run an actual breakdown for your budget?"

---

### "The bank pre-approved us for less than we need"

**Response:**
"Pre-approvals are based on stress test rates — they're conservative by design. A good mortgage broker can sometimes find products or structures that improve your qualifying amount. It's also worth looking at whether the property type matters — some lenders view properties with legal income suites more favourably because the rental offsets risk. Let's not close doors until we've explored options."

## Category 3: Representation Objections

### "My cousin / friend / family member is an agent"

**Response:**
"That's great — family connections in this business matter. A couple of honest questions: Are they active in the Brampton detached market specifically? Real estate is very local — someone who primarily does condos in Mississauga will have a different network and market knowledge than someone doing Brampton houses daily. I'm not trying to replace them — just want to make sure you have the right person for this specific purchase. What does your cousin/friend primarily work on?"

**Note:** Respect the relationship. Don't badmouth. Ask questions that surface gaps.

---

### "We don't want to sign a Buyer Representation Agreement"

**Response:**
"I totally understand the hesitation — it sounds like a big commitment. Let me explain what it actually means: a BRA means I'm legally working for *you*, not the seller. Without it, I can't share offer strategies, price analysis, or confidential negotiation tactics — because I'd technically be representing the seller's side. It's actually the thing that gives you more protection, not less. The agreement is for 90 days, and if you're not happy with how things are going, we can talk about your options. Does that help clarify it?"

**TRESA requirement:** BRA must be signed before sharing confidential strategies. Do not pressure to sign.

---

### "We already looked at some homes with another agent"

**Response:**
"Understood — I respect that. Are you in an active representation agreement with them right now? If you are, I'd never ask you to step outside that. If the relationship hasn't been formalized, it might be worth thinking about who's the best fit for this purchase. Either way, let me know if you ever need a second opinion on a listing or neighbourhood — happy to help."

**Note:** Under TRESA, must not interfere with existing representation relationships.

## Category 4: Property-Specific Objections

### "The house we liked sold before we could act"

**Response:**
"That's exactly why I want us to have a process in place before the next one comes up. Brampton's desirable properties — particularly detached with legal suites in Credit Valley and Sandringham — often get offers within 5-7 days of listing. If you're serious about a home, I need to know before it lists, not after. Let's get your must-haves locked in and your financing letter ready so we can move same-day when the right one appears."

---

### "We saw unpermitted renovations — is that a deal-breaker?"

**Response:**
"Not automatically — but it needs to be priced in and understood. Unpermitted work (especially basement suites) can create issues with: insurance, closing (some lenders require permits), and resale. The questions are: Can the work be permitted retroactively? What's the cost to remediate if it can't? And does the seller adjust price accordingly? This is something where a home inspection and sometimes a contractor walkthrough makes sense before proceeding."

**Risk note:** Never advise a client to accept unpermitted work without proper due diligence.

---

### "The neighbourhood isn't what we expected"

**Response:**
"Which part isn't fitting? Brampton is very neighbourhood-specific — Credit Valley feels completely different from Bramalea or Sandringham. If it's the commute, transit access, or community feel, let's narrow the search. Some buyers find that what they thought they wanted (newer build) vs. what actually works for them (larger lot in older neighbourhood) shifts once they see a few homes. What's the specific gap?"

---

## Category 5: Process Objections

### "We're not sure about the offer process"

**Response:**
"I'll walk you through every step before we submit anything. Here's the typical flow: I prepare a Comparative Market Analysis (CMA) so we know fair value, we decide on our strategy together (offer date vs. pre-emptive), I draft the Agreement of Purchase and Sale, and we review it together before signing anything. You're never pressured to submit — I'll give you my honest read on whether it's the right move."

---

### "We had a bad experience with a previous agent"

**Response:**
"I'm sorry to hear that — that's a frustrating experience when you're making the biggest purchase of your life. Can I ask what happened? I want to make sure I don't repeat the same mistake. What would 'working with a great agent' look like for you?"

**Insight:** Let them describe the gap. Then address it specifically.

---

## Key Principles

1. **Never badmouth competitors** — RECO Code of Ethics, Section on professionalism
2. **Never guarantee outcomes** — market direction, mortgage approval, offer success
3. **Always recommend independent professional advice** — mortgage, legal, inspection
4. **Document all disclosures in writing** — conflicts of interest, remuneration, representation status
5. **BRA is a client protection tool** — frame it that way, never as agent protection
