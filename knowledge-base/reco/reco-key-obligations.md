# RECO Key Agent Obligations — Ontario

*Source: RECO Information Guide, TRESA (Trust in Real Estate Services Act, 2002 as amended), Code of Ethics*
*For full detail, refer to official RECO materials. This is a summary for quick reference.*

## Registration Requirements

All persons trading in real estate in Ontario must be registered with RECO (Real Estate Council of Ontario) under TRESA.

- **Salesperson registration**: Requires completion of Pre-Registration Education (Humber College), articling period, and registration fee
- **Renewal**: Every two years. Requires 24 continuing education hours
- **Brokerage registration**: Separate registration required for the brokerage entity
- **Broker of Record**: Each brokerage must have a registered Broker of Record

## Duty of Disclosure to Clients

A registrant must disclose in writing to clients and customers:
1. Material facts about a property that affect the transaction
2. Any conflicts of interest
3. Remuneration received from any party to the transaction
4. Whether the registrant is representing the buyer, seller, or both (and implications of each)

## Buyer Representation Agreement (BRA)

Under TRESA, before a registrant can enter into multiple representation (representing both buyer and seller), they must:
- Disclose the situation in writing
- Obtain informed, written consent from all parties

**Important:** A Buyer Representation Agreement (BRA) must be signed before the registrant can share confidential client information or negotiate on the buyer's behalf.

Key BRA provisions:
- Duration (term of representation)
- Geographic scope
- Property type
- Remuneration terms
- Holdover clause (typically 60-90 days after expiry)

## Multiple Representation (Formerly Dual Agency)

Multiple representation occurs when a registrant represents both the buyer and seller in the same transaction.

**Under TRESA:** Multiple representation requires:
1. Written disclosure to all parties
2. Written, informed consent from all parties BEFORE representation begins
3. Limitations on what the registrant can do (cannot share confidential info, cannot advise on price strategy)

*This is a high-risk compliance area. When in doubt, refer to RECO or legal counsel.*

## Code of Ethics Key Principles

1. **Integrity**: Act honestly, with integrity and in good faith
2. **Competence**: Only provide services you are competent to provide
3. **Best interests**: Act in the best interests of your client
4. **Disclosure**: Disclose all conflicts of interest
5. **Confidentiality**: Protect confidential client information
6. **Cooperation**: Cooperate with other registrants

## Advertising Rules

Under TRESA and RECO guidelines:
- All advertising must identify the registrant AND their brokerage
- Cannot make false or misleading representations
- Must disclose if a testimonial is from a paid source
- Cannot advertise interest rates or specific mortgage products without FSRA licensing
- Social media posts are subject to the same rules as print advertising
- Team names must include brokerage name

## FSRA (Financial Services Regulatory Authority) — Mortgage Referrals

Registrants may refer clients to mortgage professionals without FSRA licensing, but:
- Cannot provide mortgage advice or rate guidance
- Cannot receive undisclosed referral fees from mortgage brokers
- Any referral fee must be disclosed to the client in writing

## Trust Account Requirements

Deposits received must be:
- Held in a trust account
- Not commingled with general business funds
- Reported on monthly trust reconciliation
- Available for RECO inspection

## Terminating a Representation Agreement

- Either party may terminate by mutual written agreement
- Upon termination, registrant must return all client documents
- Holdover clause survives termination for the specified period
- Cannot abandon a client mid-transaction without reasonable notice

## Complaints and Discipline

Complaints against registrants are handled by RECO:
1. Client files complaint with RECO
2. RECO investigates
3. Possible outcomes: no action, education/coaching, discipline hearing
4. Discipline can result in: reprimand, fine (up to $50,000), suspension, revocation

**For registrants facing complaints:** Notify your brokerage and errors & omissions insurance carrier immediately.
