About Georgetown

Georgetown is the main urban centre of the Town of Halton Hills, sitting on the Credit River approximately 40 km west of Toronto. Population 39,620. What makes Georgetown unusual in the GTA is the combination: a town with a genuine downtown, Walk Score 86, school ratings that outperform many Mississauga communities, and home prices averaging $961,778 — roughly half of Lorne Park and $200K below comparable Oakville communities.

The downtown core on Main Street is walkable — independent shops, cafés, the heritage GO station from 1858, and Dominion Gardens Park along the Credit River. This is not a strip-mall suburb pretending to be a town. Georgetown predates the GTA's suburban expansion by over a century and it shows in the bones: brick buildings, tree-lined streets, a river running through it.

The uncomfortable reality for commuters: 91% of Georgetown residents drive to work, and the GO train to Union Station takes approximately 55-65 minutes — fare zone 35, the far end of the Kitchener line. Georgetown is not a transit-first community. It earns its living on school quality, home prices, and lifestyle character — not GO proximity. Buyers who need under 40 minutes to Union should look at Mount Pleasant Brampton or Port Credit instead.

Who Lives in Georgetown

Georgetown's 39,620 residents are predominantly British/European heritage: English (31%), Canadian (29%), Scottish (24%), Irish (22%), German (10%). 85% own their home. 55% of households are families with children. Only 18% are single-person households — this is a family community.

Income profile: 15% earn over $200,000 annually, 16% earn $150K–$199K, 11% earn $125K–$149K — the upper-income segment is present but less concentrated than in Lorne Park or Kleinburg. Top industries: manufacturing (12%), retail (12%), education (9%). 31% hold a bachelor's degree or higher.

66% commute to another city — Georgetown is genuinely a bedroom community for Toronto, Brampton, and Mississauga workers. 91% drive. The 4% who take transit face a 55-65 minute GO ride. This shapes everything: Georgetown buyers are almost universally car-driving households.

Georgetown History

Georgetown was founded in 1821 by George Kennedy — an entrepreneur who settled on the Credit River banks and built several mills. The early settlement was known as "Hungry Hollow". In 1828, John Galt of the Canada Company opened the York-to-Guelph Road (now Highway 7), connecting the settlement to other Ontario communities and triggering commercial growth.

In 1837, the Barber brothers — William and James — purchased Kennedy's mills and foundry and renamed the settlement Georgetown. The brothers started the paper-making industry in 1854, using electricity produced by a Credit River dynamo — one of the earliest industrial electricity applications in Canada. Their products included wallpaper in large volumes. Berwick Hall, John R. Barber's family home, still stands at Main and Park Streets.

Georgetown GO Station opened in 1858 as a Grand Trunk Railway station — 168 years ago. The heritage station building remains in use today. GO Transit service began April 29, 1974. The Halton Hills amalgamation in 1974 absorbed Georgetown as the main urban centre of the new municipality.

Nearby Norval (within Halton Hills) was home to Lucy Maud Montgomery — author of Anne of Green Gables — from 1926 to 1935. Montgomery described Norval as "one of the prettiest villages in all Ontario."

The Mississauga Nation (Ojibwa Algonquian people) held this territory prior to 1818, when the British government purchased the land that became the townships of Esquesing and Nassagaweya.
