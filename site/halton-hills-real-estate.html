The Three Communities

Georgetown — The Main Event

Avg $961,778 · Walk Score 86 · Georgetown GO

Georgetown is the reason buyers come to Halton Hills. Population 39,620 on the Credit River. Georgetown District HS at 7.7/10 #119/747 — one of the strongest school ratings of any GTA community under $1M. Walkable Main Street. 34 parks. Heritage GO station from 1858. Named after English poet John Milton-era founder George Kennedy (1821). For the complete Georgetown guide, see the neighbourhood page.

→ Full Georgetown guide with live research data

Acton — The Value Entry Point

~$820K–$900K · Acton GO Station · Small-Town Character

Acton is Georgetown's more affordable neighbour to the west — a distinct small town with its own Acton GO Station on the Kitchener line. Acton has leather industry heritage (the Dominion Tannery operated here for generations), a walkable downtown core, and prices typically $50K–$100K below Georgetown. For first-time buyers or those prioritizing entry price over Georgetown's school rating, Acton is the calculation.

Uncomfortable truth about Acton schools: school ratings for Acton-specific catchment were not confirmed in live research. Verify at hdsb.ca before purchasing based on school access.

Glen Williams — The Luxury Rural Community

~$2,015,000 · Credit River · Arts Community

Glen Williams is Halton Hills' hidden prestige address — a small luxury community on the Credit River with an established arts scene, century-old stone homes, and average prices around $2,015,000. The community is home to artists, established professionals, and buyers who want Caledon-level privacy with Georgetown-level community infrastructure nearby. Car-dependent; nearest GO is Georgetown GO (~10 min drive).

Transit — Two GO Stations

Halton Hills has two GO stations on the Kitchener line — an unusual advantage for a municipality of 59,008 people:

  • Georgetown GO Station — 55 Queen Street, Georgetown. Heritage building (1858). ~55-65 minutes to Union Station. Fare zone 35.
  • Acton GO Station — Acton. Kitchener line. Slightly further west than Georgetown.

87% of Halton Hills residents drive to work. Transit usage is low (Georgetown HoodQ shows 4%). Halton Hills buyers are almost universally car-driving households — both cars in most families. The GO serves one commuter in the household; the other drives.

Sources: Wikipedia Georgetown GO Station (visited 2026-06-07); HoodQ Georgetown transit data (visited 2026-06-07)

Who Lives in Halton Hills

Population: 59,008 (Statistics Canada 2021 — actual 2026 figure higher given Halton Hills growth). 87% homeownership — one of the highest in Ontario. Predominantly British/European heritage: English, Canadian, Scottish, Irish backgrounds dominate in Georgetown. 13% immigrants — much lower than Brampton (58%) or Mississauga (51%). Recent immigrant percentage: 0.69% — this is not a new-immigrant destination community.

Halton Hills is a stable, established, primarily white/European-heritage community. Buyers from Brampton or Mississauga's South Asian-majority communities will find Halton Hills culturally different. There is no temple, no Hindi-language school, and minimal South Asian commercial infrastructure in Georgetown or Acton. This is not a weakness — it is an accurate demographic description that matters to buyers making family decisions.
