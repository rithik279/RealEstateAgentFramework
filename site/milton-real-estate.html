Niagara Escarpment & Conservation Areas

Milton sits on the Niagara Escarpment — a UNESCO World Biosphere Reserve — making it one of the few municipalities in Ontario where a family can hike through ancient fossil beds on a Saturday morning and catch the GO train to downtown Toronto on a Monday. Five Conservation Halton parks are accessible directly from Milton:

  • Rattlesnake Point Conservation Area — cliff-edge trails, rock climbing, Niagara Escarpment views
  • Crawford Lake Conservation Area — rare meromictic lake, reconstructed Iroquoian village, ancient fossil beds
  • Hilton Falls Conservation Area — 10m waterfall, Bruce Trail access
  • Kelso Conservation Area — beach, ski hill, mountain biking, camping
  • Mount Nemo Conservation Area — Escarpment cliffs, cedars, rare plant communities

This is the fundamental lifestyle argument for Milton over Brampton or Mississauga at comparable prices: you can walk from your backyard to a UNESCO Biosphere Reserve. Brampton buyers paying the same $990K live in a suburban grid with no natural feature of this scale accessible without a 45-minute drive.

Transit & Getting Around

Milton GO Station — 780 Main Street East, Milton. Western terminus of the Milton line. Fare zone 24. Opened October 25, 1981. Brick station building. Parking: 1,082 spaces. Travel time to Union Station: approximately 50-60 minutes. The station is 16 minutes walk from Milton's main town area (Walk Score data, walkscore.com).

Walk Score for Milton: 75/100 — Very Walkable. Higher than most 905 suburban communities — reflects Milton's walkable Old Milton downtown core and proximity to the GO station. Highway 401 passes through Milton for car commuters: approximately 50 minutes drive to downtown Toronto off-peak.

Milton History

In 1820, Jasper and Sarah Martin were granted 100 acres designated Lot 14, Concession 2, Township of Trafalgar. Martin built a grist mill along the creek and created Mill Pond to power the mill — the settlement was known as Martin's Mill. In 1837, with a population of approximately 100, the community was renamed after the English poet John Milton. The Martin and Foster families were the principal landowners, and their names still appear on Milton buildings and streets today.

The Niagara Escarpment lands were incorporated into Milton through the 1974 municipal restructuring, dramatically expanding the town's footprint and natural heritage assets. Crawford Lake Conservation Area was established to protect its rare meromictic lake (one of only a few in the world) and a reconstructed 15th-century Iroquoian village found on site.

The Mississaugas of the Credit held this land prior to 1818, when the Crown purchased the territory. Chief Ajetance agreed to the sale in the context of colonial pressure and the community's depleted state. The Halton Region Museum preserves significant agricultural heritage from the settlement era.
