Ontario Land Transfer Tax Rates 2026

Ontario Land Transfer Tax (LTT) is paid when you buy property in Ontario. It is calculated on a marginal (graduated) basis — each bracket applies only to the portion of the purchase price within that bracket, exactly like income tax.

Purchase Price RangeMarginal RateCumulative Tax at Top of Bracket
First $55,0000.5%$275
$55,001 – $250,0001.0%$2,225
$250,001 – $400,0001.5%$4,475
$400,001 – $2,000,0002.0%$36,475
Over $2,000,0002.5%—

Worked Example — $800,000 Home (Brampton, Mississauga, or other 905)

First $55,000 × 0.5% = $275

$55,001–$250,000 ($195,000) × 1.0% = $1,950

$250,001–$400,000 ($150,000) × 1.5% = $2,250

$400,001–$800,000 ($400,000) × 2.0% = $8,000

Total Ontario LTT = $12,475 — paid at closing through your lawyer.

Toronto Municipal Land Transfer Tax Rates 2026

If you are buying inside the City of Toronto boundary (not Etobicoke-as-905, not Scarborough-as-905 — the actual current City of Toronto), you pay a second full land transfer tax on top of the provincial one. Toronto MLTT uses the same graduated structure but with different bracket endpoints:

Purchase Price RangeToronto MLTT RateCumulative Toronto MLTT at Top
First $55,0000.5%$275
$55,001 – $400,0001.0%$3,725
$400,001 – $2,000,0001.5%$27,725
Over $2,000,0002.0%—

Worked Example — $800,000 Toronto Condo (Toronto MLTT on top of Ontario LTT)

Ontario LTT = $12,475 (same as above)

Toronto MLTT: First $55,000 × 0.5% = $275

Toronto MLTT: $55,001–$400,000 ($345,000) × 1.0% = $3,450

Toronto MLTT: $400,001–$800,000 ($400,000) × 1.5% = $6,000

Toronto MLTT = $9,725

Total LTT for Toronto buyer = $12,475 + $9,725 = $22,200

vs. same home in Brampton/Mississauga: $12,475 (Ontario only). Toronto doubles your tax bill.

First-Time Buyer Rebates 2026

Ontario First-Time Buyer LTT Rebate — up to $4,000

First-time home buyers in Ontario receive a full rebate of Ontario LTT on the first $368,000 of a purchase price (since Ontario LTT on $368,000 equals exactly $4,000). For homes over $368,000, you still get the full $4,000 rebate applied against your total Ontario LTT — you just pay the portion above.

Toronto First-Time Buyer MLTT Rebate — up to $4,475

First-time buyers purchasing in the City of Toronto receive a separate Toronto MLTT rebate of up to $4,475. This applies on top of the Ontario rebate. Combined maximum for Toronto first-time buyers: $8,475 ($4,000 Ontario + $4,475 Toronto).

Rebate Qualification Rules (Both Ontario and Toronto)

  • Canadian citizen or permanent resident at the time of purchase
  • At least 18 years old
  • Never owned a home anywhere in the world — not just in Ontario, not just in Canada
  • Must occupy the home as principal residence within 9 months of closing
  • If purchasing with a spouse/partner: neither of you can have owned a home anywhere in the world (for the Ontario rebate). Toronto's rebate has the same requirement.
  • Applies to new construction, resale, and vacant land with a home to be built

The Brampton / 905 Advantage

Every buyer purchasing outside the City of Toronto boundary — in Brampton, Mississauga, Vaughan, Milton, Halton Hills, Caledon, Hamilton — pays only Ontario LTT, not Toronto MLTT. At $800,000, that is $9,725 less in land transfer tax on closing day compared to buying the same property inside Toronto.

That $9,725 in 905 savings has to be funded from somewhere — in Toronto, it comes out of your cash at closing. It is real money that cannot be financed through your mortgage. If you are weighing a Toronto condo vs. a Brampton condo at similar price points, the LTT difference alone is a meaningful part of the equation.

When Is Land Transfer Tax Paid?

Land transfer tax is paid on closing day — the day you take possession. Your real estate lawyer collects it and remits it to the province (and to Toronto if applicable) when registering the deed in your name. You cannot defer it, put it on your mortgage, or pay it in installments. It must be in your lawyer's trust account before keys are handed over.

First-time buyer rebates are applied automatically at closing — you do not pay first and claim back. Your lawyer applies the rebate and you pay only the net amount.

Other Closing Costs to Budget

  • Legal fees — $1,500–$2,500 for a standard purchase. More for complex transactions.
  • Title insurance — $200–$400. Required by most lenders; protects against title fraud and survey issues.
  • Home inspection — $400–$600. Technically optional but strongly recommended, especially in the 905 where many homes are 20–40 years old.
  • CMHC mortgage default insurance — required if down payment is less than 20%. Ranges from 2.8% (10–14.99% down) to 4.0% (5–9.99% down) of the mortgage amount. Added to your mortgage principal, not paid at closing.
  • Property tax adjustment — if the seller has prepaid property tax past the closing date, you reimburse them the proportional amount at closing.
  • HST on new construction — resale homes have no HST. New construction includes HST but it is usually included in the purchase price (check your agreement).

As a rough guide for 905 buyers: budget 1.5%–2.5% of purchase price for total closing costs excluding LTT. On an $800,000 home in Brampton, that is $12,000–$20,000 out of pocket at closing, plus $12,475 in LTT (or $8,475 if first-time buyer). Total closing cash needed: roughly $20,000–$32,000 before your down payment.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is Ontario land transfer tax calculated in 2026?
Graduated marginal rate. 0.5% on first $55K; 1.0% on $55K–$250K; 1.5% on $250K–$400K; 2.0% on $400K–$2M; 2.5% above $2M. Each rate applies only to the portion of price within that bracket — exactly like income tax brackets. Use the calculator above for instant results.
What is the first-time home buyer land transfer tax rebate in Ontario?
Up to $4,000 on Ontario LTT. If your purchase price is $368,000 or less, you pay zero Ontario LTT. Above $368,000, you still get the full $4,000 rebate subtracted from your total. Must have never owned a home anywhere in the world. Must be 18+, Canadian citizen or PR, must occupy as principal residence within 9 months. Applied automatically at closing — no separate claim needed.
Does Toronto have an extra land transfer tax?
Yes. Inside City of Toronto boundary only. Toronto MLTT rates: 0.5% on first $55K; 1.0% on $55K–$400K; 1.5% on $400K–$2M; 2.0% above $2M. Toronto first-time buyers get up to $4,475 Toronto rebate on top of the $4,000 Ontario rebate ($8,475 total). Buying in 905 (Brampton, Mississauga, etc.) means no MLTT — only Ontario LTT.
When do you pay land transfer tax in Ontario?
On closing day. Your real estate lawyer collects it and registers the deed. Must be paid in full before keys transfer. Cannot be deferred, financed, or added to your mortgage. First-time buyer rebates are applied at closing — you pay net, not gross.
What other closing costs should I budget for in Ontario?
Legal fees ($1,500–$2,500), title insurance ($200–$400), home inspection ($400–$600), CMHC premium if under 20% down (2.8%–4.0% added to mortgage), property tax adjustment, and moving costs. Budget roughly 1.5%–2.5% of purchase price for non-LTT closing costs. On $800K in Brampton: $12K–$20K + $12,475 LTT ($8,475 if FTB).
