About Mississauga

Mississauga is Canada's sixth-largest city with over 720,000 residents, occupying 292 km² of land on the western shore of Lake Ontario — immediately west of Toronto. It's part of Peel Region and has operated as an independent city since amalgamation in 1974, incorporating Port Credit, Streetsville, and several other former communities into a single urban municipality.

Unlike Toronto's neighbourhoods, which developed organically over a century, most of Mississauga was purpose-built from the 1960s to the 2000s as suburban master-planned communities. The result is a city where each neighbourhood has a distinct identity: Port Credit feels like a lakeside village; Lorne Park is a century-old estate enclave with 100-foot lot frontages; Erin Mills is a planned family suburb with good school access; City Centre is a downtown core that grew up around Square One.

The practical case for buying in Mississauga: direct GO train access to Union Station in 27–35 minutes (Lakeshore West line from Port Credit and Clarkson), a major employment base (Pearson, pharma cluster, Microsoft HQ, Amazon, Walmart Canada HQ), 480+ parks, and Lake Ontario waterfront access that Toronto's western suburbs don't offer.

Mississauga Neighbourhoods

Mississauga has 26 neighbourhoods across four broadly different character zones:

Waterfront & South Mississauga (Premium)

  • Port Credit — Mississauga's most walkable village, Credit River mouth, GO train, avg sold $1,291,844. 74.9% ownership. Families with kids only 26% — primarily couples and professionals.
  • Lorne Park — Ranked #2 of 26 Mississauga neighbourhoods. Avg list $2,929,000. 100-year tree canopy. LPSS school boundary drives property values. Fastest luxury GO commute: Clarkson GO to Union in 24 min.
  • Mineola — Established estate neighbourhood between Port Credit and Cooksville. Avg $2M+. Limited inventory.
  • Clarkson — More accessible entry point to south Mississauga. Avg ~$1,050,000. Clarkson GO serves Lorne Park and Clarkson buyers.

Central Mississauga (Family Suburbs)

  • Erin Mills — Planned family community, avg ~$1,150,000. Strong school access. Credit Valley Hospital nearby.
  • Churchill Meadows — Newer development, strong South Asian community, family-oriented, townhomes and semis dominate.
  • Streetsville — Village character within Mississauga. Heritage main street. Streetsville GO station.
  • Meadowvale — Well-established suburban community. Kitchener GO line access at Meadowvale GO.

City Centre Corridor (Condo-Dense)

  • City Centre / Square One — Highest condo density in Mississauga. Avg condo ~$650K. Hurontario LRT under construction will change walkability permanently.
  • Cooksville — More affordable entry point. Avg ~$925,000 detached. Cooksville GO station (Milton line).

Schools: Peel District School Board

Transit: GO Train, MiWay & the Hurontario LRT

  • Lakeshore West GO line: Port Credit GO Station, Clarkson GO Station. Express service to Union Station 27–35 minutes. The fastest 905 commute to downtown Toronto available in Mississauga. Peak-direction trains run every 15 minutes during rush hour.
  • Milton GO line: Meadowvale GO, Lisgar GO, Cooksville GO. Commute to Union Station approximately 45–55 minutes.
  • Kitchener GO line: Malton GO, Bramalea GO (Brampton border). Commute to Union approximately 50–60 minutes.
  • MiWay (Mississauga Transit): City-wide bus network. Connects to Brampton Züm, TTC at border stations, and all GO stations.
  • Hurontario LRT (under construction): Light rail transit line running from Port Credit GO north along Hurontario Street to Brampton Gateway Terminal. When complete, will add permanent rapid transit to the City Centre corridor and connect to the Milton and Kitchener GO lines at Cooksville.

Source: GO Transit gotransit.com; Metrolinx Hurontario LRT project updates; MiWay mistonline.ca
